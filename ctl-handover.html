Things left off on purpose

Seven absences that look like gaps and are not. Each one is a decision, and each one is reversible the moment the underlying fact changes.

No review stars in search markup

Google stopped showing star ratings for businesses that mark up reviews about themselves on their own site back in 2019. Adding the code anyway does not produce stars — it risks a penalty across the whole domain. Your stars in search come from the Google Business Profile, which already works.

No monthly payment figures

The estimator exists and is switched off until the financing terms are real ones. A number a customer reads on your site is a number they will expect at signing.

No individual town pages

Sixteen towns are listed on one areas page rather than spread across sixteen near-identical pages. Thin town pages with nothing local on them are the pattern search engines demote. They become worth building once there are case studies to put on them.

No certification badges

Nothing is claimed that has not been confirmed — no FORTIFIED badge, no manufacturer certifications, no licence number. Empty is recoverable. Wrong is not.

No Facebook review feed

Meta shut off the recommendations API entirely in September 2025 — there is no longer any way to pull them automatically, and scraping the page breaks their terms. So the ten on the site were copied by hand and are dated as such. Refreshing them stays a manual job.

No live careers form yet

The application form is finished and tested, and the page is switched off until the upload service is deployed. A form that accepts a résumé and quietly discards it is worse than no form: the applicant believes they applied, and nobody ever tells them otherwise. The same rule governs the contact form, which refuses to submit rather than losing an enquiry.

No cookie tracking

Analytics are cookieless and only load if the visitor agrees. That keeps the consent banner honest and the privacy policy short.

How it runs

The site is a set of static files — no database, nothing to patch on a Tuesday. It is designed to be hosted on Cloudflare Pages, which serves it free at the traffic a regional contractor sees, and it loads fast because there is very little to load.

One exception, and it is worth knowing about. A file cannot be sent to a set of static files, so the résumé upload on the careers page needs a small piece of software running on Cloudflare. It does one thing — take an application, check it, and put the file in private storage only you can reach — and it is the only moving part on the whole site.

Content lives in plain, structured files rather than being buried in the page code, which means the reviews, the town list, the services and the gallery can all be edited without touching the design. If CTL later wants a proper editing interface, that plugs into the same files.

Running cost is close to nothing — hosting is free at this scale, the form service has a free tier, and the only per-visitor cost is the Google reviews lookup, which is worth watching for the first week and then forgetting about.

The current site is untouched. Nothing has been deployed, no DNS has been pointed anywhere, and ctlpro.com is running exactly as it was. Going live is a deliberate, scheduled step, and it should not happen before the redirect map in item 13 exists.
