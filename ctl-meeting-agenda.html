CTL Pro Construction LLC · Working session

One sitting,everything cleared.

The site is built. What stands between it and ctlpro.com is not more building — it is twenty-nine things that need a decision, a login or a signature, and almost all of them need you specifically. This is the order to work through them in.

Nothing here takes long on its own. The reason it is worth doing in one sitting is that most of these items unlock each other, and chasing them one email at a time is what turns a two-week launch into a two-month one.

Three things to start before anything else

Everything else on this list can be done in the room. These three cannot — they involve somebody outside it, and they take days. If the session starts by kicking these off, they run in the background while the rest gets worked through.

1. The attorney. Four documents need a read: the terms, the privacy policy, the six questions on the job application, and the language on the storm page about how CTL works a claim. That last one is the important one — in Louisiana, negotiating a claim on a homeowner's behalf is public adjusting and needs a licence. The page is written so CTL documents the damage, meets the adjuster and scopes the repair, and the homeowner files and negotiates. If that is not exactly how you operate, the wording has to change.

2. The Google Business Profile. Confirm who owns it. If nobody at CTL knows, the reclaim process starts today — Google takes days to a couple of weeks, and it is on the critical path for the reviews on the site.

3. The old site's address book. We need a list of every page on the current ctlpro.com before it comes down, plus a copy of its DNS settings. Both are only obtainable while it is still running. This one is ours to do — we just need to know who hosts it.

Block A — Accounts and who owns them

The unglamorous half of a launch, and the half that decides whether CTL owns its own website afterwards. Every account below should be in CTL's name with us added to it, not the other way around.

Seven logins

Bring these, or create them in the room.

  1. 01

    The domain registrar login for ctlpro.com

    Wherever the domain itself was bought. We need to confirm it is unlocked and that the transfer code can be retrieved.

  2. 02 Blocks the launch

    A copy of the current DNS settings

    From whoever hosts the site now. It is a list, and they can usually export it in a click.

    Why it matters more than it sounds — the same settings that point ctlpro.com at a website also point your email at your mailbox. Moving the site without copying those across first stops company email arriving. Not slows it. Stops it. This is the single most likely way to make a launch day go badly, and it is completely avoidable.

  3. 03

    A Cloudflare account in CTL's name

    This is where the site will be hosted. Created under CTL's email with us added as a member — so if we ever part ways, the site does not leave with us.

  4. 04

    A GitHub account for whoever edits the site

    The photo editor signs in with one. Free, and it takes two minutes, but each person who will add gallery photos needs their own.

    Worth saying plainly — this is the one piece of friction in the setup. If asking office staff to hold a GitHub account is a problem, say so in the session and we will look at a different editor instead.

  5. 05 Start today

    Ownership of the Google Business Profile

    The listing that shows up in Maps and in the sidebar when somebody searches "roofer Lafayette". We need manager access to point it at the new site and to pull your reviews in.

  6. 06

    Google Search Console

    Free, and it is how we tell Google the new site exists and watch for anything broken afterwards. Created against the same Google account as the Business Profile.

  7. 07

    Who hosts the site now, and when does that bill end

    Do not cancel it on launch day.

    Keep it running 30 days past the switch. If anything goes wrong, going back is a five-minute change — but only while the old site still exists to go back to.

Block B — Decisions only you can make

Seven choices. Each one switches on something that is already built and currently sitting dark. None of them needs research beforehand — they are questions about how CTL actually works.

Seven questions

Each one switches on something that is already built and sitting dark.

  1. 08 Blocks the launch

    Where do résumés go?

    The careers page is live in the menu and the application form is built. What is not settled is where the CVs land. Right now that would be a private storage bucket we run — which works, but it means we are holding strangers' personal documents on CTL's behalf. For a company your size that responsibility is better sitting with a company whose actual business is holding them.

    OptionWhat it costsWhat it gets you
    A hiring platform A monthly fee, on CTL's account They hold the CVs, handle how long they are kept, and give you a place to track applicants. Most responsibility moved off both of us
    A form service A small monthly fee Keeps the application form exactly as it is on the site; the file goes to them instead of to us. Cheapest change
    Indeed only Free The careers page becomes a page that points at your Indeed listings. Nothing to run, nothing to hold — but you lose the branded application

    Until this is settled the form tells applicants to email the office instead. That is deliberate — a form that appears to take somebody's CV and quietly drops it costs them a job they think they applied for.

  2. 09

    What do you use to track jobs and customers?

    JobNimbus, AccuLynx, Roofr, Leap — or a whiteboard and a phone. There is no wrong answer; we just need to know, because the site already collects every enquiry into one list and can push it straight into whatever you use.

    If the answer is "nothing yet" that is fine and nothing is lost. Enquiries are stored from day one, and the first time a system is connected, everything collected up to that point gets delivered into it.

  3. 10 Blocks the launch

    Which inbox should leads go to — and who is the second pair of eyes?

    Every assessment request emails somebody. We need the address, and we want a second one on it.

    One mailbox is a single point of failure. The most common way a website loses a job is that the enquiry arrived correctly and nobody opened it that week.

  4. 11

    Do you want to know which ads are producing calls?

    Call tracking gives a different phone number to the website, Facebook and Google so you can see which one rings. It costs a monthly fee and it is genuinely useful — or we skip it and every number on the site stays your real one, which is what it is today.

  5. 12

    Which visitor statistics do you want?

    Two choices. A paid one that does not track people across the internet and needs no cookie banner nagging visitors, or Google's free one, which is more detailed and connects to Google Ads if you ever run them. Either way the site asks permission before it loads anything.

  6. 13

    The financing partner and the real terms

    Partner name, the actual rates and terms, and the pre-qualification link.

    The payment calculator stays off until these are real. A monthly figure on a website is a number a customer will hold you to, so it is not going up as an estimate.

  7. 14

    ctlpro.com or www.ctlpro.com?

    One is the real address and the other forwards to it. Thirty seconds to decide, awkward to change later. Both work for visitors either way.

Switches something on

  1. 21

    Your Louisiana contractor licence number

    No licence line prints anywhere on the site until this exists.

  2. 22

    Are you FORTIFIED-certified?

    One of the photographs shows a FORTIFIED sign, but we could not verify the certification, so the photo was excluded and no badge is claimed.

    Worth confirming — in Louisiana it is an insurance-discount badge, which makes it one of the stronger trust signals available to a roofer here. Same question for any manufacturer certifications.

  3. 23

    Are you hiring, and for what?

    Job titles, full time or seasonal, what each role does day to day, and the genuine minimum somebody needs. Until this arrives the careers page runs as a general application, which is honest but weaker — it cannot name a job that might not exist.

  4. 24

    Sixteen are listed now, grouped by parish. Confirm, add or remove.

Block D — Sign-offs

Two items, both of which need somebody other than us to read something. Start them the day of the session; they are the longest pole in the tent.

Two reads

One by a lawyer, one by you. They are checking different things.

  1. 25 Start today

    Your attorney reads four things

    The terms of service, the privacy policy, the six questions on the job application, and the claims section of the storm page.

    The claims section is the one that matters. Louisiana licenses public adjusting, and the line between documenting damage and negotiating a claim is a legal one. The page is written to stay on the right side of it, and the hiring questions are written conservatively — but careful is not the same as cleared, and neither of those is a judgement we are qualified to make for you.

  2. 26

    You read the storm page yourself

    Separately from the attorney, and for a different reason: does it describe how CTL actually handles a storm job? The first 48 hours, what you do when the adjuster is on site, what you will and will not do during a claim. It is written from how the business appears to work. Tell us where it is wrong.

Block E — The working arrangement

Worth settling in the same sitting, because it changes what the launch looks like and who is responsible for what afterwards.

Three, none of them technical

The only items on this list that are about the business rather than the site.

  1. 27

    Payment terms

    What is owed, when, and how. Invoices will come through Stripe.

    One practical note — paying an invoice by card carries a percentage fee that gets meaningful at five figures, while paying by bank transfer through the same invoice costs a few dollars flat. Same link, different button. Worth knowing before the first one arrives.

  2. 28

    Who looks after it after launch

    A site with forms, a photo editor and a live reviews feed is not a thing you hang on a wall. Somebody needs to be the person who gets called when a form stops working on a Saturday. Decide what that arrangement is — response time, what is included, what is billed — rather than discovering it during the first outage.

  3. 29

    Who pays the running costs

    Hosting is essentially free at this size. The recurring costs that do exist are the ones chosen in Block B — the hiring platform, the customer system, call tracking, statistics — plus a small per-visitor charge from Google for the live reviews. That last one is the only thing on the site that bills by traffic, so we will set a spending alert on it the day it is turned on.

What happens after the session

So the timeline is clear before anyone commits to a date. The switch itself takes minutes; the two weeks before it are what make those minutes uneventful.

  1. Same day — attorney review starts, Business Profile ownership confirmed, and we take a full copy of the current site's pages and settings while it is still up.
  2. Week one — every account and key from Blocks A and B gets wired in. The site goes onto a private test address with everything switched on, and we work through it properly: submit the real form, book a real appointment, apply for a real job, check the enquiry arrives in the right inbox and the right list.
  3. Week two — you review it on that test address. Content from Block C gets added as it arrives. Legal comes back and gets applied.
  4. The switch — your email settings are recreated and verified first, then the address moves. We send and receive a test email on ctlpro.com before calling it done. Old page addresses forward to their new homes so nothing you have ever linked to breaks.
  5. The week after — Google is told about the new site, we watch for anything broken, and whoever is maintaining the gallery gets walked through it. The old site stays paid up for a month, so going back stays a five-minute job if it is ever needed.

The short version

Bring to the session

The registrar login. Whoever hosts the site now, so we can ask them for the settings export. The Google account that owns your Business Profile. Your contractor licence number. Your attorney's details. CTL's legal name and EIN for the accounts we set up together.

Decide in the session

Where résumés go. What you use to track customers. Which inbox gets leads, and who else sees it. Call tracking, yes or no. Which statistics. The financing partner. And whether the address is ctlpro.com or www.ctlpro.com.

Two that stop it working

Only two items genuinely prevent the site doing its job: an inbox key so the contact form can send, and somewhere for résumés to go. Everything else degrades gracefully — a section hides itself rather than showing a gap.

One that can go wrong quietly

The DNS settings from your current host. It is the difference between a launch nobody notices and a morning where company email has stopped and nobody knows why. It is a five-minute request, and it needs making before anything moves.
