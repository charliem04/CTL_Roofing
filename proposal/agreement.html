What "launch" means

One defined moment, used by every clause in this document: the production build is live on ctlpro.com, DNS has been cut over, forms deliver to CTL, and the site is serving real visitors. Not a preview link. Not a staging URL.

What is already built

Twenty-one pages, rendering, on real CTL content — 40 job photographs, nine named faces, ten reviews reproduced word for word, sixteen towns. The remaining work is accounts, decisions and sign-offs, not construction.

Payment mechanics
  • Invoices are issued electronically. Bank transfer (ACH) costs a few dollars flat; card payment carries a percentage fee that becomes meaningful at five figures — same invoice, different button.
  • Instalments are due on the same calendar day each month. Payments more than 14 days late pause non-emergency retainer work until cleared; emergency response is never withheld over an invoice.
  • Ownership of the site — code, configuration and build tooling — transfers to CTL Pro Construction LLC on receipt of the final build payment. Photographs, copy, reviews and the CTL name are CTL's throughout and always were.
  • Third-party running costs (domain, any CRM, hiring platform, call tracking, the per-lookup Google reviews charge) are billed by those vendors directly to CTL and are not part of any figure on this page.
Two points to confirm at signing The instalment term (6, 9 or 12 months) and the qualified-lead target on page 7 are the only figures in this document not already settled in conversation. Both are written here as proposals with recommended defaults, and both are one line to change.

02 — Scope of the build, continued

The engineering underneath

Six things a customer never sees, every one of which decides whether the site earns anything.

Built to stay up

Static site on Cloudflare's global network — no database to patch, nothing to hack on a Tuesday, free to serve at the traffic a regional contractor sees. Every internal link is verified at build time; a broken link fails the build rather than reaching a customer.

Leads that arrive

A dedicated form service records every assessment request, notifies CTL, and can hand the lead straight to a CRM. Spam is filtered before it reaches an inbox. A second service takes résumé uploads on the careers page into private storage only CTL can open.

Editable without a developer

Reviews, towns, services, team and gallery live in plain structured content files, plus a photo manager at /admin/. Changing a phone number or adding job photos does not mean touching design code.

Found in search

Sitemap, robots, structured business data, per-page titles and descriptions, clean URL structure, Open Graph cards for when a link is shared. Deliberately no fake review markup — Google penalises it, and CTL's stars already come from the Business Profile.

Fast and accessible

Self-hosted fonts, processed images, measured performance pass, no layout shift. Keyboard navigable, screen-reader labelled, contrast checked — the legal exposure a contractor's site quietly carries, closed.

Launch and handover

DNS cutover run to a written T-14 → T+7 plan with a rollback, plus a documented set: go-live checklist, credentials register, production readiness audit, route map, CMS guide. CTL is never locked to one person.

Left off on purpose

Four absences that look like gaps and are not — each a decision, and each reversible the moment the underlying fact changes.

Not on the siteWhy not
Review stars in search markup Google stopped showing stars for businesses marking up reviews about themselves in 2019. The code produces no stars and risks a penalty across the domain. CTL's stars come from the Business Profile, which already works.
Monthly payment figures The estimator is built and switched off until the financing terms are real ones. A number a customer reads on the site is one they will expect at signing.
Sixteen separate town pages One areas page instead. Thin town pages with nothing local on them are the pattern search engines demote — worth building once there are case studies to put on them.
A Facebook review feed Meta retired the reviews API. The recommendations are reproduced on the page and the tab is linked — a live feed is not something anyone can build any more.

05 — The monthly retainer

What $300 a month covers

A site with forms, a photo manager and a live reviews feed is not a thing you hang on a wall. The retainer answers "who gets called when a form stops working on a Saturday" in advance, rather than during the first outage.

Included every monthWhat that means in practice
Hosting & infrastructure Cloudflare Pages and both background services kept configured, deployed and healthy. Hosting itself is free at CTL's traffic; this covers the hands on it.
Uptime & form monitoring The site and — more importantly — the lead path are watched. A form that silently stops delivering is the failure that costs real money, so it is checked, not assumed.
Security & updates Dependency and framework updates, security headers maintained, spam filtering tuned, TLS and DNS records kept correct.
Content edits — 2 hrs Text changes, new gallery photographs, team and town updates, hours, phone numbers, service copy, seasonal storm messaging. Unused time rolls forward one month (4 hrs maximum).
Bug fixes Anything delivered in the build that stops behaving correctly is fixed at no charge, for as long as the retainer runs. Not a 90-day warranty that quietly expires.
Monthly report One page: leads received, where visitors came from, top pages, search queries CTL appears for, page-speed health. The same numbers the bonus is judged on — visible monthly, not revealed at month six.
Backups & rollback Every change version-controlled. Any bad change can be reversed to the previous live state in minutes.

Response times

  • Site down, or forms not delivering — acknowledged same day, worked until resolved, including weekends after storms.
  • Something broken but not blocking leads — within 2 business days.
  • Content edits and requests — within 3 business days.

Terms of the retainer

  • Month to month. Either side may end it with 30 days' written notice.
  • Ending it does not affect the build balance, the bonus, or CTL's ownership of the site.
  • Work beyond the included hours is quoted before it starts — never billed as a surprise.
  • Additional hours, when wanted, are billed at $85/hr proposed
Why it starts at hosting and not before Charging a care fee for a site nobody can visit would be charging for nothing. The retainer keeps a live site healthy, so the first $300 falls due the day the final production build is serving on CTL's domain — not one day sooner, however long the remaining sign-offs take.

06 — The performance incentive

What "performs well" has to mean

A $2,000 bonus judged on a feeling is a $2,000 argument. So it is judged on five measurements CTL can read in the monthly report, from sources neither side controls.

#CriterionMeasured fromTarget at month 6
1Qualified leads Form records + tracked calls, averaged across months 4, 5 and 6 ≥ 10 per month confirm
Or 2× the pre-launch baseline, whichever CTL prefers to set at signing.
2Conversion rate Qualified leads ÷ unique visitors, same three months ≥ 2.0%
Industry median for contractor sites sits near 1–2%.
3Search visibility Google Search Console Organic clicks in month 6 ≥ 150% of month 1
4Speed & experience Google's own field data, mobile, 75th percentile LCP ≤ 2.5s · INP ≤ 200ms · CLS ≤ 0.1 — "good" on all three
5Reliability Uptime and form-delivery monitoring ≥ 99.9% uptime, no lead-path outage left unresolved past 24 hours

How it pays

$2,000  — criterion 1 met, and at least three of criteria 2–5.

$1,000  — criterion 1 met, fewer than three of the rest.

$0  — criterion 1 not met. The site's job is leads; everything else is in service of that.

Assessed once, in the 30 days after month 6, and payable within 30 days of that written assessment — one evaluation, not a rolling target.

What counts as a qualified lead

A real person in the service area making a genuine enquiry about work — through the assessment form, the storm line, or a tracked call originating from the site.

Not counted: spam and bot submissions, vendor and marketing solicitations, duplicates from the same person inside 30 days, job applications (tracked separately), obvious tests, and anyone plainly outside Acadiana.

Every submission is timestamped and stored, so the count is a record both sides can open — not a claim either side makes.

Fairness clauses — both directions

THE BONUS IS NOT EARNED BY

  • Traffic, impressions or rankings alone — the target is enquiries, not applause
  • Leads CTL sourced elsewhere that the site merely happened to be live for
  • Paid advertising CTL funds separately, unless both sides agree to include it

MONTHS EXCLUDED FROM THE COUNT

  • Any month where analytics, call tracking or the reviews key was not yet live — nothing to measure
  • Any month where CTL asked for the site, a form or a page to be held back
  • A named storm that materially disrupts CTL's operations — the window extends by that month
